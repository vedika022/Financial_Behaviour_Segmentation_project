What the business should do

  • Protect Premium Customers

    16% of customers, ~36% of income×spend value. Priority for loyalty, premium cards, and relationship managers.

  • Convert high-income low spenders

    Budget-Conscious + High-Income Savers earn a lot but spend little. Fit for deposits, investment plans, and targeted offers.

  • Watch high-spend mid income

    Affluent Moderates spend like premiums on a smaller income. Good for rewards, but monitor over-extension.

  • Engage Low Spenders

    Mid income, spend score ~16. Activation campaigns beat new-acquisition cost.

Data-quality notes from the audit

  • • 2,000 rows × 8 columns. Only Profession is missing (35 rows, 1.75%) — filled with the mode (Artist) before clustering.
  • • Clustering used MinMax-scaled Annual Income and Spending Score only.
  • • Correlations between numeric fields are all ~0. Age vs income 0.02, income vs spend 0.02 — that is why two-feature clustering still finds groups.
  • • Work Experience has 5 outlier points; Age includes 0 (data error). These were not used as clustering features.
  • • Artist is the most common profession (612); Homemaker is the least (60).

How to read model health here

This project is unsupervised, so there is no accuracy or ROC-AUC. Quality is judged by cluster separation (silhouette, Davies–Bouldin, Calinski–Harabasz) plus whether the groups are usable for marketing. K-Means with 7 groups beats GMM on those three scores in the final comparison file. Segment names come from each cluster’s mean income and spend — cluster IDs stay in the saved CSV for the model.
