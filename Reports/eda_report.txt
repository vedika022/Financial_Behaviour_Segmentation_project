1> Univariate Analysis

Age
The customers' ages range from approximately 1 to 100 years.
The distribution is relatively spread across the age range, with noticeable concentrations around the 20–40 and 50–70 age groups.
There does not appear to be a single dominant age group.

Annual Income
Annual income ranges from approximately $0 to $200,000.
The majority of customers have annual incomes between approximately $50,000 and $150,000.
Customer counts are relatively low in the lower-income range, particularly below $50,000.
The distribution shows several areas of higher concentration rather than a single clear peak.

Spending Score
Spending scores range from 1 to 100.
A substantial concentration of customers has spending scores between approximately 40 and 60.
Higher spending scores, particularly around 80–100, also have relatively high representation.
The distribution therefore appears to contain customers with both moderate and high spending behaviour.

Gender Distribution
Female data is more than male data 

Profession 
Artist is the most repeating profession among the customers
Home maker is the least common 

Work Experience
Work experience ranges from approximately 0 to 17 years.
The distribution is concentrated toward the lower end, with the majority of customers having 0–2 years of work experience.
There is another noticeable concentration around 4–10 years, although with lower frequencies.
Very few customers have more than 10 years of work experience.
Overall, the distribution is right-skewed, with a relatively small number of customers having high work experience.

Family Size
Family size ranges from approximately 1 to 9 members.
Customers with 2–4 family members make up a substantial portion of the dataset.
Family size of 2 has the highest frequency.
The number of customers generally decreases as family size increases.
Very few customers have 8–9 family members.


2> Bivariate Analysis 

1. Annual Income vs Spending Score
Customers are distributed across a wide range of income and spending scores.
There does not appear to be a strong linear relationship between annual income and spending score.
Customers with similar income levels can exhibit substantially different spending behaviours.
This suggests that income alone does not determine spending behaviour, which makes this relationship particularly relevant for customer segmentation.

2. Age vs Spending Score
Spending scores are widely distributed across almost the entire age range.
There is no clear linear relationship between age and spending score.
Customers of different ages can have both low and high spending scores.
Therefore, age does not appear to be a strong standalone predictor of spending behaviour based on this visualization.

3. Age vs Annual Income
Annual income is widely distributed across most age groups.
There is no obvious strong linear relationship between age and annual income.
Higher-income customers can be found across a broad range of ages.
This indicates that age and income provide relatively independent information in this dataset.
